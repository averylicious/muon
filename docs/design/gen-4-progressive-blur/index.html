Muon with backdrop glass

Drawn from the Gen 3 baseline (RC .1420 on the Pixel 8). Android 17 QPR2 (API 37.2) lets a surface blur whatever is drawn behind it, live, with RenderNode.setBackdropRenderEffect. First, a new choice for the navigation bar; then the surfaces that could turn to glass, ranked. Switch the device to see what an older phone gets, scroll the lists under the glass, tap the tabs, and drag the A–Z handle. The blur is the browser's approximation, and the songs are fictional.

A choice of navigation bar, and of what it's made of

Edge to edge stays the default: today's Material bar with a softer top edge. Floating is opt-in and turns the mini player and the tabs into glass capsules, either stacked or, with Tuck in while scrolling on, sliding into one row as you scroll down. A second choice sets the surface for every bar and toolbar. Frosted blurs what's behind it. Tinted shows the content through the bar's own colour with nothing blurred, so it costs no more than a plain bar. Solid is opaque, in Material You or Muon colours, whichever palette is chosen. Both choices live in Settings › Appearance, and every phone on this page follows them.

Where it doesn't fit

Song actions sheet and ⋮ menus

Sheets, dialogs and dropdown menus open in their own windows, and a backdrop only sees its own window. Muon already blurs the app behind the sheet from the app's side (13-song-actions), and that stays. Compose 1.13's blurBehindRadius on DialogProperties is the system route if it's ever wanted.

Now Playing's glow

The cover glow is blurred once per song from a 40-pixel copy of the cover, so it costs as much as drawing a picture. A live backdrop would look the same and re-blur every frame.

The player while it grows

A backdrop is cut off by any fading layer above it, and the mini player's morph into Now Playing fades. The growing panel keeps its Frost layer, whichever navigation bar is chosen.

Landscape

Sideways, the tabs become a rail and the player a side panel (landscape-01). Nothing scrolls under them, so the navigation bar choice applies to portrait only.

What building it would take

Floating bar, on every phone

  • No 37.2 needed. Frost already draws a blurred copy of the tab behind the bars, and it can clip that copy to capsules, so floating glass works from Android 12. On 37.2 the backdrop can take over if it measures better.
  • The choice is one more preference beside Pure black. The list already leaves room under glass bars (LocalUnderBars); the floating height just changes that number.
  • Stacked is the floating default; Tuck in while scrolling is a switch under it, so both ship.

Bar surface

  • Frosted, Tinted and Solid are one preference that every bar, toolbar, search pill and snackbar reads. Tinted and Solid skip the blur entirely, which is the answer to the cost note below.
  • Solid takes the chosen palette's container colours (Material You or Muon), so it needs no colours of its own.
  • The Blur switch narrows to Now Playing's cover and the blur behind the player and song menu.

Backdrop setup

  • compileSdkMinor = 2 in app/build.gradle.kts (it is 0 today), and a check of SDK_INT >= 36 before SDK_INT_FULL.
  • No Compose backdrop modifier exists yet, even in 1.13. One small helper owns a platform RenderNode and draws it with drawRenderNode under each surface.
  • Every glass surface follows the existing Blur switch and has the solid fallback shown here.

Progressive edges

  • BlurRadiusSpec comes with Compose 1.13 (BOM 2026.11.01, Kotlin 2.2+, compileSdk 37.1). That upgrade is its own slice, done first.
  • Not yet checked: how a BlurRadiusSpec reaches the platform backdrop. Read the 1.13 sources before relying on it; a uniform blur works without it.

Synced lyrics

  • Lyrics in focus needs timed lines. Muon reads plain lyrics_text from Tauon today. Not checked yet: whether Tauon keeps LRC timestamps it could pass on.
  • The line blur itself is plain Modifier.blur (Android 12+), independent of the backdrop work.

Cost

  • The renderer re-samples and re-blurs a backdrop every frame, with no cache. Small, brief surfaces (toolbar, snackbar, bubble) are safe bets; full-width bars need a Pixel trace.
  • Early Haze benchmarks found its native-backdrop path used more CPU per frame than its copy-based one, which is the approach Frost already takes.
